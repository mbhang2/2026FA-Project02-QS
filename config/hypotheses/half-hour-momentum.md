# Hypothesis: Same-Day Intraday Momentum in NQ Futures — First 30 Minutes Predicting the Last 30 Minutes

Does the direction of NQ price movement in the first 30 minutes of a session predict the direction of movement in the final 30 minutes of that same session? The canonical "market intraday momentum" effect, documented in equity markets, proposes that early-session returns continue into late-session returns — a same-day continuation rather than a reversal. The hypothesis under test is that this effect exists in NQ futures at the 30-minute window scale and survives the friction of real execution: the spread paid on entry and exit, and the per-side commission charged on each trade.

Two competing accounts make different predictions. The first holds that intraday momentum is a genuine feature of market microstructure and order flow: early-session price movement reflects information or behavioral patterns that persist through the day, so a positive first-half-hour return predicts a positive final-half-hour return, and vice versa. The second holds that any such effect has been arbitraged away in modern NQ futures, or that it is too small relative to transaction costs to be tradable. Under this account, the first-half-hour return contains no predictive power for the final-half-hour return after paying the cost of entry, exit, and the bid-ask spread.

The observation that discriminates these accounts is a systematic tendency for the session's final-30-minute return to share the sign of its first-30-minute return across many sessions. Defined against the session's own clock: "first 30 minutes" is the interval from the session's first bar to thirty minutes in, and "last 30 minutes" is the interval ending at the session's recorded close (which for a short session is shorter than a full afternoon — the window is the final half-hour the session actually has). A strategy trading this signal enters on the close of the first half-hour in the signal's direction and exits on the close of the final half-hour, so the claim includes the spread and commission of a one-way entry and exit. If over many sessions the final half-hour shows no directional tendency to follow the first, or the tendency is too weak to clear the friction of one round trip, the hypothesis is falsified.

This hypothesis does not address whether the effect is stronger in certain volatility states or time periods, does not require a regime filter, and is not searching for a reversal. It asks the simple question first: at this data's granularity, on real NQ trades, does the first half-hour point the way the last half-hour goes?
