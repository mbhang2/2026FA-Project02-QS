# Task 66 — FWER of the conditional null under `max_of_n`, at the N we operate

**Pre-registered before anything ran.**

## 1. The question

Task 65 established that the **rotation** null is calibrated per candidate --
`z = (observed - null mean)/null std` has mean -0.041, std 1.048, and 4.0% of
candidates beyond +/-1.96 -- where the **sign-flip** null sits 1.81 sigma low
with 45% beyond -1.96 and none above.

That makes Task 64's remedy plausible: stop letting the cheap null decide, route
to the conditional one. It does **not** make it established. Per-candidate
calibration is necessary and not sufficient. `max_of_n` over 50 nulls is a
further step with its own behaviour, and Task 64 is the standing demonstration
that a null which looks fine in one measurement can be badly wrong in the gate.

**So: what is the FWER of `rotation` + `max_of_n` at N = 50, under the current
metric?** Task 38's 0.060 was `conditional` + `max_of_n` at N = 10 and predates
the idle charge. Nothing describes what routing would actually produce.

## 2. The measurement

**C = 100 campaigns x N = 50 trials x R = 150 rotations = 755,000 runs.**

| knob | value |
|---|---|
| campaigns `C` | 100 |
| trials per campaign `N` | 50 |
| rotations per candidate `R` | 150 |
| candidates | `NullGenerator(seed=66_001)` |
| dataset | `SyntheticSpec(n_sessions=175, ticks_per_session=390, vol_per_tick=0.15, signal_strength=0.0)` |
| dataset seed | `4_242 + campaign_index` — varies per campaign, as in Task 64 |
| rotation seeds | `800_000 + (campaign*N + trial)*R + r` |
| metric | `score(..., idle_charge=0.18)` |
| correction | `correction.max_of_n(..., b=10_000, seed=17, q=0.05)` |
| accept | `reject AND observed_best > 0` |

**R = 150 and not less, deliberately.** `max_of_n` carries a measured resampling
bias of +0.011 at R = 50 and nothing distinguishable from zero past R = 150. At
q = 0.05 a +0.011 bias is a fifth of the thing being measured. Paying for R = 150
removes a known confound from a measurement whose whole purpose is to find out
whether an unknown one exists.

**C = 100 is a coarse read and is meant to be.** At a true 0.05 it gives a Wilson
interval of roughly [0.022, 0.112] -- enough to separate "near nominal" from
Task 64's 0.0020 or from something badly anti-conservative, and not enough to
pin a value. If the answer lands near nominal, extending C is a later decision
and the harness resumes from what is on disk.

## 3. Runs are in-process, and that is a deliberate substitution

Task 64 and 65 ran every candidate through `RemoteStrategy`, spawning a
sandboxed child per run. Here the strategy is executed in process via
`Engine.for_test`.

**Why it is legitimate.** The sandbox exists to keep *agent-written* code away
from the harness. These candidates are generated by `NullGenerator`, which this
repository wrote. There is nothing to isolate from.

**Why it is safe.** Blotter digests were compared between the two paths on the
first four candidates of the stream and came back **byte-identical**, at a 3.8x
to 5.2x speed difference. `--stage verify` repeats that comparison on a larger
sample and prints it; a single mismatch invalidates the substitution and the
measurement should be re-run remotely at four times the cost.

**What it buys.** 755,000 runs at ~2 s each is over 100 hours. At ~0.25 s it is
roughly 11 hours, which is one night.

## 4. What gets reported — fixed now

1. **`reject_rate`** — campaigns where `max_of_n` rejects.
2. **`accept_rate`** — rejects **and** best observed score positive. The FWER.
3. The **paired contrast** against Task 64: both are C x N = 50 campaigns of 50
   edgeless candidates under the same metric and the same correction, differing
   only in the null. Task 64's sign-flip arm gave 0.0020.

Also recorded as context, not as tests: the p-value distribution, the fraction of
campaigns with a positive best, and unscorable counts.

**What each outcome would mean, stated before the numbers exist:**

- **Near 0.05** — routing is sound, and Task 64's conservatism is a property of
  the cheap null rather than of the correction.
- **Also far below 0.05** — the problem is in `max_of_n` at N = 50, not in the
  null. Routing would not fix it, and the correction itself becomes the subject.
- **Materially above 0.05** — rotation + `max_of_n` over-rejects, which is the
  dangerous direction and would take precedence over everything else in this
  file.

## 4a. R = 50 as a paired second arm — added before running, 2026-09-23

Michael asked why not R = 50 as well. The answer is that it costs **nothing**:
R = 50 is a prefix of R = 150, so taking the first 50 rotations of each candidate
gives a second arm on the same candidates, the same rotations and the same
observed best, with only the null sample size moving.

That is the contrast `correction.resampling_bias` claims to describe -- +0.011 at
R = 50, zero past 150 -- and it was measured "on known normal nulls". This
measures it on the nulls we actually use. Task 38 ran the same paired contrast
for `conditional` and found the predicted improvement did not appear: R = 50 gave
0.0575 and R = 150 gave 0.0600, moving the estimate *up* by one campaign in four
hundred.

Reported for both arms: `reject_rate`, `accept_rate` with Wilson intervals, and
the **discordant counts** -- campaigns accepted at one R and not the other --
which is where a paired contrast carries its information.

## 6. The result — 2026-09-23

**FWER 0.0600, CI [0.0278, 0.1248], at R = 150.** 100 campaigns x 50 trials x 150
rotations, 755,000 runs, 7.69 hours, zero unscorable trials.

| arm | accept | 95% CI | reject | p median |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rotation, R = 150 | **0.0600** | [0.0278, 0.1248] | 0.0600 | 0.439 |
| rotation, R = 50 | 0.0700 | [0.0343, 0.1375] | 0.0700 | 0.415 |
| sign-flip (Task 64, C = 1,000) | **0.0020** | [0.0005, 0.0073] | 0.0020 | **0.9997** |

Campaigns with a positive best: 85 of 100.

### The correction is fine. The null was the whole problem.

Same N, same `max_of_n`, same metric, same generator, same idle charge. The only
thing that differs between the top row and the bottom is which null the
candidates are judged against, and the error rate moves **thirty-fold**.

This is the first outcome section 4 named: *near 0.05 -- routing is sound, and
Task 64's conservatism is a property of the cheap null rather than of the
correction.* It rules out the second: the problem is not `max_of_n` at N = 50.

Two things corroborate it rather than merely agreeing with it. The p-value median
is **0.439** where sign-flip's was 0.9997 -- roughly uniform, which is what a
calibrated null looks like from the outside. And 0.060 is the **same figure Task
38 measured** for `conditional` + `max_of_n`, at N = 10 and before the idle
charge existed. Two different N, two different metrics, the same rate.

### R = 50 against R = 150, and the bias grid holds

`correction.resampling_bias` predicts R = 50 over-rejects by **+0.011** relative
to a large R, measured there on known normal nulls. Here, on the nulls we
actually use: **+0.010**.

That is a close agreement and it should not be over-read. The arms are paired --
same campaigns, same rotations, only the prefix length differs -- and the
information in a paired contrast is in the discordant campaigns, of which there
were three: one accepted only at R = 150, two only at R = 50. **McNemar exact,
two-sided: p = 1.0000.** The point estimates move in the predicted direction and
the paired evidence cannot distinguish the difference from zero.

Task 38 reached exactly this position on the same contrast for `conditional` --
one discordant campaign, McNemar p = 1.0000 -- and the honest reading is the same
now as then: R = 150 is retained because the bias is *known to be zero* there
rather than because R = 50 was shown to be worse.

### What this establishes, and what it does not

**Establishes:** `rotation` + `max_of_n` at the N and the metric we operate
controls FWER at approximately the nominal rate. Routing candidates to the
conditional null rests on a measured property rather than an assumed one, and
Task 64's 0.0020 is attributable to the sign-flip null alone.

**Does not establish:** a precise value. C = 100 was chosen as a coarse read and
the interval [0.028, 0.125] is consistent with 0.05 and also with 0.10. It
separates near-nominal from 0.0020 decisively and pins nothing. Extending C is a
later decision and the harness resumes from disk.

**Does not establish power**, which is the reason to route and was already
measured: Task 59 puts `round_turn_mc` at 80% power for a signal strength of
0.287 against the conditional null's 0.242, and at strength 0.15 the two are
0.100 against 0.533. That contrast, not this one, is the case for routing.
