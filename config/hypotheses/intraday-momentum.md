# Hypothesis: Intraday Momentum Continuation in NQ After Large Single-Bar Moves

**Does a single bar with an unusually large price move — measured against that session's own recent bar-to-bar volatility — tend to be followed by continuation in the same direction over the next 2–5 bars, or by reversal?**

The brief asks whether large intraday moves keep going or give back. The papers present conflicting evidence. Christensen et al. (2006.08307) model intraday momentum as a latent state that persists across bars, suggesting continuation is real but masked by filter lag. Mesfin (2605.04004) tests fourteen momentum signal families on NQ itself and finds none survive realistic friction — all fail either because gross returns fall below the 2-point cost floor or because they lack year-to-year stability. Lis et al. (2602.18912) find that machine learning can extract predictable structure from overreactions at ultra-short (1–5 minute) horizons, but that "classical behavioral momentum effects dominate at intermediate frequencies, particularly around 10 minutes." This suggests a real effect exists but is narrow in time and fragile to execution.

The hypothesis narrows to a concrete claim: after a bar closes with a move larger than the session's recent typical bar size (operationalized as the median absolute close-to-close change of the prior N bars in that session), the next 2–5 bars will more often continue in that direction than reverse. This is testable without machine learning, without side information, and without assuming a regime shift. It does not require the move to be the largest of the day, only large relative to what that session has been doing. Entry and exit are part of the claim — a strategy must buy or sell the bar after the large move closes, and hold or exit within the next few bars, paying the spread on both sides and the 0.1-point commission per side.

What this is NOT asking: whether the effect is stronger at certain times of day, whether it interacts with volatility regime, whether it survives a regime filter, or whether it is driven by order flow imbalance (no order-flow data exists). It is not asking whether the move itself is predictable, only what happens after it has occurred. It is not a test of gap continuation, opening-range breakouts, or any multi-day pattern — the move and the test window are both intraday.

The papers leave open whether a simple, friction-aware momentum rule on NQ can clear the cost floor when defined narrowly enough (session-relative size, short holding window, no regime conditioning). Mesfin's negative result on fourteen families does not rule out a fifteenth; Lis's finding that 10-minute momentum is stronger than 1-minute suggests the answer may depend on bar size in a way the prior art has not isolated. This hypothesis tests that gap.
